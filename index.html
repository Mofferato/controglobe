The premise

One change, applied consistently: the historical trajectories of the Global North and the Global South are transposed, beginning around 500 BCE. Everything else — geography, climate, rivers, mountains, the sea lanes — stays exactly where it is. The point of divergence is not a battle or a birth but a long reversal of who industrialises, who colonises, who is colonised, and who writes the textbooks.

Rules of the setting

  1. Geography is fixed. Only history moves. Deserts stay deserts; the Nile still flows north.
  2. Borders are internal. No frontier in this world descends from a partition conference. They follow rivers, escarpments, watersheds and the settlements of internal wars.
  3. Roles, not costumes. A nation carries another's historical role, not its names, language or religion. Congo is Germany's role in Kikongo.
  4. Custom entities are allowed where nothing maps cleanly — Aïr and Kanem exist because the Alps and Sápmi have no single counterpart.
  5. Contradiction is expected. This is open source. Forks and competing canon are the point.
