Africa — The European Swap

Every African nation and its Global-North counterpart, with the revised non-colonial border set. Africa carries Europe's history; the borders below were drawn by African states themselves, not by any conference.

Border doctrine

Main article: United States of Arabia. Further information: Controglobe: the premise

Border doctrine. No frontier on this map descends from a partition conference. There are no meridian or parallel borders — the Libya–Chad, Egypt–Sudan, Algeria–Mali and Niger–Chad straight lines of OTL do not exist. Frontiers follow rivers, escarpments, watersheds, dune-seas, linguistic lines and the settlements of centuries of internal wars, exactly as Europe's did.

Southern Africa = Southern Europe

Seven of these were one country. The Union of Zambezia — Zimbabwe, Zambia, Botswana, Namibia, Malawi, Lesotho and southern Mozambique — was the Controglobe Yugoslavia, and it came apart in the wars of the 1990s.

The islands — outside the European swap

Under the Controglobe rule that Oceania trades places with the Indian Ocean, Macaronesia and the Atlantic islands, these are Pacific-analogue states, not European ones: Cape Verde, the Canaries, Madeira, São Tomé & Príncipe, the Comoros, Seychelles, Mauritius and Réunion. The two exceptions run the other way: Iceland is Morocco and Greenland is Cyprus.

What changed from the first draft

Niger and Chad are no longer stand-ins for existing countries. Niger is a cantonal Alpine confederation; Chad is a herders' nation on the Sápmi model, with borders defined by migration routes.

Angola is Italy, not North Macedonia — the south needed a great power, and Macedonia moved to Malawi.

Mauritania is Norway and Libya is Finland, which puts the Nordic-analogues in the right order west to east.

Mozambique is two countries: Moldova in the north, Montenegro in the south.

Kong (France) does not cover the whole west. Sierra Leone, Liberia and Tem exist as independent duchies because no centralising monarchy ever finished the job.

Every frontier is redrawn. No straight lines, no conference borders — the Sahara is divided along dune-seas and wells, the Congo basin along watersheds, the south along the 1990s ceasefire lines.
